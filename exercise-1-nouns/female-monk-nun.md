# Monastics

## Gendering

A female monk is generally called a nun, especially in Christian traditions. In **Buddhism**, a _female_ monk is called a **bhikkhuni**, while a _male_ monk is a **bhikku**.

The term "_monk_" is often used informally **for both male and female monastics** from other backgrounds.

### Religious contexts

  <b>Christianity</b>:  The <i>female equivalent of a male monk is a nun</i> who dedicates her life to religious service in a convent or monastery.

  <b>Buddhism</b>: A female monastic is a <i>bhikkhuni</i>. The male equivalent is a <i>bhikku</i>. <u>The term "nun" is also commonly used for a bhikkhuni in English</u>. While the two are often the same, in some contexts there are nuances between male and female monastics, as well as historical and ongoing challenges for women seeking full ordination in certain traditions.

<h3>Roles and practices </h3>
<b>Similar duties</b>: Like male monks, nuns often take vows and dedicate their lives to spiritual practice, contemplation, and service.

Specific traditions: Some Buddhist traditions have specific rules or practices for nuns, which can vary by lineage and country.

<b>Examples of practice</b>:
Female monastics in various traditions may engage in meditation, study, and community life, sometimes leading to the establishment of communities specifically for nuns, as seen with some Western monasteries and those in Asia.
